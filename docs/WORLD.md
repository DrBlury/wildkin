# WILDKIN — world & design bible

This is the reference for the game's story, science, names, creatures and
moves. Code, art and dialogue should follow it. (The old working title was
MONQUEST; the game is now **WILDKIN — The Brimming Storm**.)

---

## 1. The premise in one paragraph

The creatures of the Vale are **kin**. A kin is an animal built around a
**kernel**: a pebble-sized living crystal that holds a **condensate** — a cloud
of billions of identical light-matter particles (villagers call them
**motes**) all sharing one quantum state. The condensate is the kin's life
force: it shapes and powers the body, and every kin keeps it topped up by
soaking up one kind of energy (heat, sunlight, static, moving water...) —
that is its **type**. A kernel can only hold so much. Over-full kin **brim**:
the surplus leaks out as heat waves, static storms, frosts and floods. When two
kin clash in a **bout**, every blow knocks motes loose; the loose energy
thermalises into the land as warm soil, gentle rain and fixed nitrogen, and a
kin whose condensate falls below threshold simply **dozes** until it refills.
Nobody is hurt, and the weather stays kind. That bargain — people guide the
bouts, give kin names, food and a **lantern** to rest in; kin share their
strength and keep the seasons gentle — is the **Kinship**. People who travel
with kin are **wardens**.

## 2. The science (the in-world explanation, kept close to real physics)

Everything below is written so it *could* be true with today's physics. The
Lorebook teaches it chapter by chapter.

**Motes are polaritons.** In real laboratories, light trapped between two
mirrors mixes with excitations in a crystal to make *exciton-polaritons*:
half-light, half-matter particles about a hundred thousand times lighter than
an electron. Because they are so light and are *bosons*, huge numbers of them
can pile into one shared quantum state — a Bose-Einstein condensate — even at
room temperature in the right materials. Such condensates leak light all the
time and must be continuously *pumped* to survive ("driven-dissipative"
systems), they flow without friction, form vortices, and can even behave like
a solid and a superfluid at once (a *supersolid*). A kin's kernel is a natural
mirror-walled microcavity that grows such a condensate.

**Types are pump bands.** Each kernel absorbs one kind of energy efficiently:
BLAZE — infrared/heat; BLOOM — visible sunlight via chlorophyll-like pigments;
SPARK — electric fields and charge; TIDE — pressure waves and flow in water;
STONE — strain in rock (piezoelectric crystals turn squeezing into charge);
GALE — air pressure and wind; BRAWL — strain in the kin's own bones
(real bone is piezoelectric, so every punch and jump pumps it); FROST — pumps by *shedding* heat (anti-Stokes
optical cooling, which really does refrigerate crystals with laser light, so
frost kin chill their surroundings); VENOM — chemical energy of rot and
fermentation (chemiluminescence); DREAM — the faint bioelectric fields of
other living minds; DUSK — broadband absorbers that drink even starlight,
which is why they look darker than shadows; SWARM — many tiny kernels
flashing in sync (*superradiance*); BEAST — ordinary metabolism, weak but
steady; WYRM — ancient kernels so large they couple to whole weather systems.
Matchups follow physics: water quenches heat, charge races through water,
stone grounds charge, heat melts frost, and so on.

**HP is coherence.** A blow is one condensate's field striking another's; it
scatters motes out of the shared state. The scattered energy leaves as heat,
light and ions — ions seed raindrops, lightning fixes nitrogen for plants. At
0 HP the condensate drops below its threshold and collapses to a tiny seed
state: the kin **dozes**, cool and limp, but unhurt. Re-pumping wakes it.

**Status problems are field instabilities.** BURN = runaway heating in the
condensate; POISON = impurities scattering motes every turn; NUMB = the field
phase-locked by an outside charge so it can't move freely; SLEEP = the pump
has stalled; FROZEN = the condensate stuck in a metastable state.

**Lanterns record, they don't squash.** A lantern's heart is two mirrors of
**heartglass** with an extremely high quality factor — a cavity that can hold
a condensate for years with almost no loss. A kin's identity lives in its
kernel's quantum pattern, not in its flesh. When a calm kin *mode-matches*
into the cavity, the kernel and condensate slip inside; motes are bosons, so
all of them fit in a single mode a few microns wide. The rest of the body —
mostly water, carbon and silica held in shape by the field — is shed as a puff
of glittering mist. On release, the lantern pumps the kernel and the
re-inflated field pulls matter from the air and ground, precipitating the body
around itself in a second or two, the way a snowflake grows around its seed.
That is the flash when a kin comes out, and why a kin let out in a desert is
always thirsty.

**Why catching needs a tired kin, and why it is consent.** Only a condensate
near its ground state can transfer into the cavity without decohering. A
fresh, excited, struggling kin is far too noisy to mode-match; a tired,
sleepy or frozen one is calm. And a kin can always *refuse* by driving its own
field out of tune — which is what "it broke free" means. Better lanterns
(higher *finesse* mirrors) accept noisier, livelier states.

**Growth is a phase transition.** Each pump-and-spill cycle adds layers to the
kernel crystal. Past a critical size its pattern reorganises into a more
complex symmetry and the body re-precipitates in a bigger form: the kin
**grows**. A **shard** is a seed crystal of one symmetry; touching it nucleates
the transition at once, the way a speck of ice freezes supercooled water.

**Hearths and healing.** The Old Hearth, and every Hearth Hall since, is a big
broadband resonator — warm stone, mirrors and firelight — that pumps every
kernel near it. People gathered around hearths because kin did.

**The Lantern Shelf network is quantum teleportation.** Hearth Halls keep
kernels in lanterns on racks. To send a kin to another shelf, the hall uses a
pre-shared pair of entangled **twin crystals** plus a message over the
telegraph wire; the state is destroyed here and rebuilt there. It can never be
faster than the wire (real physics forbids it), and every transfer uses up a
twin pair, so halls grow new crystals every night.

**Weather.** A brimming wild kin leaks its surplus; a big one leaks a storm.
DRAKORA's kernel is centuries old and so large it couples to the whole sky;
a hundred years without a bout has left it brimming with a storm that could
drown the Vale.

## 3. Why the things you do are okay

| Question | Answer in the world |
| --- | --- |
| Why do kin fight? | Bouts are how kin spill surplus. It is play, exercise and weather control in one; kin want bouts the way a sheepdog wants to run. |
| Why fight wild kin? | A wild kin that comes at you is **brimming** and asking for a bout. Answering is good manners and good for the land: "every bout is a bucket of rain," as farmers say. Ignoring brimming kin is how wildfires start. |
| Does it hurt? | Blows are field on field. At 0 HP a kin's condensate has collapsed and it **dozes**; re-pumping wakes it. |
| Is catching okay? | Kin choose. Only a calm kin can mode-match into a lantern and any kin can refuse. Released kin rebuild their bodies and go home. |
| Why do kin grow? | Phase transition of the kernel; shards nucleate it. |
| What if nobody holds bouts? | Surplus pools. Before the Kinship the Vale burned and flooded; DRAKORA's last brimming drowned three valleys. |

## 4. The story of this game

**Setting.** MAPLE VILLAGE, a farming village at the south end of the Vale.
In the plaza stands the **Old Hearth**, the stone resonator where the first
wardens swore the Kinship. The village keeps the **Almanac** — the record of
every kin, season and storm — at the ALMANAC HOUSE.

**Hook.** It is the day of your **Kindling**, when a young villager receives a
first kin and becomes a warden. But the sky has been grumbling for weeks,
and the old Almanac pages say that is how it began last time: DRAKORA, the
**Stormwyrm**, has not had a bout in a hundred years and is brimming.

**Beats.**

1. *Storybook intro* (new game): the Kinship in a few pages.
2. You wake at home; a note sends you to the ALMANAC HOUSE.
3. **Keeper Linden** holds your Kindling: choose FLARIX, AQUAPO or DANDELAMB.
   You get lanterns, tonics, the ALMANAC and your **LOREBOOK**.
4. Explore three routes out of the village — WHISPER MEADOW (north),
   BRAMBLEWOOD (east) and MIRROR LAKE (west). Wardens there challenge you on
   sight. People share lore; it goes into your Lorebook.
5. **Warden Marlo** runs the **Bout Ring** in the village. Beat Marlo to earn
   the **RING SASH**.
6. With the sash, Keeper Linden sends you to the **Stormstone** at the top of
   Whisper Meadow to answer DRAKORA's challenge.
7. DRAKORA descends. Win the bout (or befriend it with a lantern) and the storm
   spills harmlessly into the Vale: the sky clears and the village
   celebrates. The Keeper asks you to finish the Almanac.

Until the storm is calmed the Vale is darker and thunder flashes now and
then; afterwards the sky is bright.

## 9. Look & feel rules

- Nothing iconic from other franchises: no red-and-white balls, no red caps,
  no nurse with pink hair and a heart, no heart emblem on the healing
  building.
- Items lying on the ground are small **satchels**.
- The player wears a teal scarf and a travel hat; the Tender is an older person
  in an apron; the Hearth Hall emblem is a flame.
- Battles open with a lantern-light wipe, not a double flash.

## 12. Quality-of-life features

- Options: text speed, battle animations on/off, sound on/off, follower on/off.
- Hold A or B to speed up text.
- Run with B from the start.
- XP for the whole team (bench members get half) and XP for befriending.
- Move menu shows whether a move strikes a weak spot on the current foe.
- L in battle throws your best lantern.
- Wild HUD shows a lantern icon if you have befriended that species.
- Keeper Linden can re-teach forgotten moves.
- LANTERN SHELF from the START menu (TWIN CRYSTAL).
- HUSH BELL keeps wild kin away.
- Summary shows temperament, trait, potential grades, size, bond, where met.
- Lorebook: every piece of lore you hear is kept, sorted into chapters.
